Hostageness is Joy!

The concept of being held hostage to the Other’s suffering should be understood as a metaphysical fact that precedes absolutely any thematization of the Other-as-a-Sufferer so that the primordial source of the hostageness is the alterity of the Other. One implication of this view is that being-hostage-to-the-Other-who-is-suffering is no longer about identifying those who are suffering more against those who do not suffer but instead a metaphysical absoluteness that connects the fecundity of the I to ALL Others primordially. In such a framework the cutting of humans into thematized categories becomes even more obviously artificial horrible breaking of joy itself.

Primary considerations

Entire essay is founded on Emmanuel Levinas’s convincing and appealing the critique of Western Philosophy as a totalizing enterprise. For Emmanuel Levinas Western Philosophy fails when it prioritizes Being as Being-to-the-World-with-Others instead of Being-to-the-Other-with-Worlds. Being-to-the-Other-with-Worlds can be explicated as the way one considers the ethical relationship prior to metaphysical knowledge. This is a radical and difficult move especially for we who are educated in the virtues of objective knowledge and absolute truth but for Levinas it is essential to bringing peace. 

> How is peace possible? Exit from totality, transcendence towards the Other! How to rejoin the Other after two thousand yrs of domination of philosophies of identity? Levinas urges us to inverse the tendency: listen to the call of peace prior to the call of truth. In the practice of philosophy, that means wisdom at the service of love instead of love at the service of wisdom” (Barszczak, p37) 

If wisdom is to be in the service of love then that means that I care about love before I care about the truth. This love is the love of the Other with great fecundity. In Levinas’s major work Totality and Infinity he introduces and justifies fecundity by the obvious state that a father feels for his son. A love that is undelinatable from the father to the son. The child is both absolutely the Other and absolutely the I. There is no breaking this contradiction and thus no breaking me from my Other. There was no decision or power previous to the relation. The father does not choose to love the son. The fecundity is metaphysical, it just is. From this it becomes obvious that (although Levinas is not so direct) there is no metaphysical basis for separating one’s connection to any Other. 

In Aristotelian terms this means that all people are ethically connected by a priori properties of being a human. But this is just a claim that must be proved within Aristotelian metaphysics that are dry and cold. “Show us the Logic of ethical relationships and describe scientifically fecundity and we’ll be ethical!” says the Aristotelian crowd. How does one make a blank face animated or an the ethically dead language love? There is no secret formula or else there would be no lack of love in the world. Common ‘scientificish’ language loses significant (if not principal) definition (compared to ethical metaphysics). This is one of the challenges of writing about Levinas: the language that is used in contemporary philosophical debates is rooted so firmly in Aristotelian metaphysics that any writing about Levinasian metaphysics is not a trivial exercise.

Returning to the concept of unlimited fecundity this does not mean that for Levinas that the I and the Other are all the same.

> The relation with the Other does not nullify separation...the relation between me and the other commences in the inequality of terms, transcendent to one another, where alterity does not determine the other in a formal sense… (Totality, p251)

For without separation there is no birth of reasoned communication (and Reason itself) from discourse. This reason-producing discourse is only possible because of the I’s passivity to the Other in the form of apology. 

> An order common to the interlocutors is established by the positive act of one giving the world, his possession, to the other, or by the positive act of one justifying himself in his freedom before the other, that is, by apology. Apology does not blindly affirm the self, but already appeals to the Other. It is the primordial phenomenon of reason, in its insurmountable bipolarity. The interlocutors as singularities, irreducible to the concepts they constitute in communicating their world or appealing to the justification of the Other, preside over communication. (Totality, p252)

Although I’ve read this passage enough that it seems obvious in its purport I will reiterate it in my terms. Because the I and Other are absolutely connected and absolutely separate logical thinking / reasonable attitudes / basically common sense come from discourse where the I apologizes to the Other for not understanding the Other’s logos. Then if the Other is tyrannical the I must accept/deny the Logos and if not the Other teaches me and I respond.

These two concepts thumbnail summarized as ‘love before truth’ and ‘reason from discourse’ underwrite the entirety of the rest of this essay and should be accepted by the reader. (at least in the first generous reading that I deserve please) Given this it is possible to approach correctly the question of hostageness. The thumbnail critique of Levinas is that being hostage to the Other requires a complete giving up of oneself to those in society that are in need. Firstly this conception of hostage and the thematization of the suffering Other will be shown to be erroneous. Then a better thematization of the Other will be presented (or re-presented because this is an interpretation the presented in Levinas’s writings) and then the implications of the thematizations will be considered. For Levinas any text when reread is alive in a new context and so it’s difficult to have confidence in the conclusions of one’s said of the saying. Yet reading and writing about the reading is a fine risk that we here take.

First use of ‘hostage’ and why the Other is not defined as the beggar, orphan, etc

Levinas first uses the word ‘hostage’ in Otherwise than Being (the term is never explicitly used in Totality and Infinity) to describe what must be true given that to be disinterested in someone is a serious move. When person A says “I don’t care about that person B” there is a shock, a confusion, an unnatural feeling that illicites the response/question “What on Earth happened to make you not care about person B?” The state of disinterestedness is shocking because it’s artifical to the natural gravity/care all Others have for Others.

> By anticipation let us ask: does not this gravity, where being's esse is inverted, refer to this pre-original language, the responsibility of one for the other, the substitution of one for the other, and the condition...of being hostage which thus takes form? (Otherwise, p6) 

It is important to note that no empirical facts about the Other have been considered here. In Totality and Infinity the Other is described as “the face of the poor, the stranger, the widow, and the orphan” (Totality, p251) but this description lives inside an explnation of what ethical space the Other exists in. In the full quote it is obvious that the poor/stranger/widow/orphan description is a necessary poetic/religious move to ensure that Otherness is never excluded from anyone and not a definition nexus for any description of the Other in its suffering.

> The Other que Other is situated in a dimension of height and abasement-*glorious abasement*; he has the face of the poor, the stranger, the widow, and the orphan, and **at the same time**, of the master called to invest and justify my freedom (Totality, p251) (bold and italics mine)

This does not only about how the empirically poor/stranger/widow/orphan is in particular the Master, it is about all Others are low and high. 

The Western Philosophical tradition has no problem understanding who ‘the master’ is: it is the king, the oligarchy, the one who speaks, the decider of justice, the enslaver (sometimes the one enslaved is oneself). But what in Western Philosophy is the bottom of humanity? When the truth comes before love the bottom is arbitrary so that it can be the slave in one conversation or the head of household in another. Is it the case that only tyrants can have conversations with other tyrants and so all their citizens are on the bottom? By this way the kings fight wars and the definitions of words are what the victorious tyrant says over the vanquished. But do these wars bring out Levinas value? No. Levinas is absolutely attacking this conception that there can be an arbitrary bottom in western thought. He uses the poor/stranger/widow/orphan description to move kingship to every Other. In Aristotelian metaphysics the opposite of the word ‘master’ is ‘slave’. In Levinasian metaphysics the opposite of the word ‘master’ is ‘silent’. Every Other qua Other is master and silent. 

Where the Other is most importantly the marginalized suffering people

It is possible, when reading Levinas, to over-thematize the Other-as-a-Sufferer. If the Other is suffering I must take the food out of my mouth and give it to the Other! A more literal explication is to say that when I am walking down the street I have an absolute metaphysical duty, as hostage to the Other, to respond to the cries of pain. If I don’t I’m failing my ethical duty, an ethical duty that is never ending. I shouldn’t sleep well at night! Given that I must sleep it is impossible to always be Levinasian. 

Is the Other really Suffering only when they are hungry? When they need me? Levinas is clear that there is a duty “...the duty to give to the other even the bread out of one's own mouth and the coat from one's shoulders.” (Otherwise, p55) but what about the Other that obviously has enough food and a thick enough coat? The position I am critiquing is the position that we know or must know who is suffering enough to require my coat. I will show that qualifying suffering is a late empirical exercise in Levinasian metaphysics, unworthy of dismissing the Levinasian song. To explicate this critique I will present a thematization (and also gross simplifications) of a supporter and a critic of Levinas that both make faulty moves.

> A supporter of Levinas could say that the ethical relation is absolute and always giving to the Other and that the Other is suffering while the I is strong in the dwelling. The supporter laments with shame that the requirement of a dwelling forces the supporter to stop giving. There is always more to give away to elevate the suffering of the Other.

> A critic of Levinas (could be the same person) could say that the ethical relation is absolute and always taking from the I. The Other is suffering while the critic is strong in a dwelling enjoying the elemental of the earth. The infinite suffering of the Other threatens the space needed to breath and there is always more that the Other wants.

> Consider the scenario of the beggar on the street. For the supporter the beggar is suffering immensely while surrounded by houses that allow others to have dwellings. The beggar’s suffering is without doubt and within the face to face discourse I rediscover the continual truth that the beggar as Other is master over me and not just silent. The the critic the beggar is suffering from a lack of dwelling, in a community of dwellings where all are suffering and take from the I. There is only so much the I can give to the beggar, only so much mastery that can be allowed. Consider the case of the drug addict Other: the Other’s suffering and needs are so absolutely endless and violence from the Other so possible and/or likely that the ethical relation must be tightly controlled so as not to hurt the I’s dwelling. If a healthy family is considered part of the dwelling then any threat of violence becomes obviously problematic.

#### The best thematization of the Other

I have just returned from speaking with a beggar (I haven’t) and the experience of discourse was reason-producing (I imagine it could be). This has made me ready and motivated! (I actually am!) The next step is to consider how thematizations are used and whether how it is possible to do the least harm that can be done. Firstly the most obvious will be considered.

The first Thematization: The Other as absolutely other

This is the power core of Levinas’s philosophy: the great and expansive concept that there is no basis for knowing what the Other is going to be. In a self-reflective sense this is cumbersome and/or shocking in that it is known that the Other is never to be completely known even though its a constant thing in language to describe the Other as known (ie. “That person is bad”) but Derrida for one found resonance with the concept.

> the thought of Being not ethical violence, but it seems that no ethics—in Levinas’s sense—can be opened without it. Thought—or at least the precomprehension of Being—conditions (in its own fashion, which excludes every ontic conditionality: principles, causes, premises, etc.) the recognition of the essence of the existent (for example someone, existent as other, as other self, etc.). It conditions the respect for the other as what it is: other. Without this acknowledgment, which is not a knowledge, or...without this “letting-be” of an existent (Other) as something existing outside me in the essence of what it is (first in its alterity), no ethics would be possible. (Derrida, p172)

With this concept the greatest possible thematized respect for the Other is declared. (Hopefully this will inspire the rest of the paper)

A second thematization of the Other is that the Other has Desire

Desire is introduced in the second paragraph after the introduction in Totality and Infinity as a principle of living with infinite appetite and so the principle that motivates our interest in the Other. This clearly assumes that it is impossible to be disinterested which makes sense when it is considered that living means doing new stuff even if that means only talking about the new weather. To allow for living to be without desire is nonsensical. And this means that living cannot be without movement towards other people. 

> No journey, no change of climate or of scenery could satisfy the desire bent toward it. The other metaphysically desired is not "other" like the bread I eat, the land in which I dwell, the landscape I contemplate, like, sometimes, myself for myself, this "I," that "other." I can "feed" on these realities and to a very great extent satisfy myself, as though I had simply been lacking them. Their alterity is thereby reabsorbed into my own identity as a thinker or a possessor. The metaphysical desire tends toward something else entirely, toward the absolutely other. (Totality, p33)

This Desire is not the interest in Aristotelian objects (absolute never-changing things) but instead in exploring the social reality in all its changing forms. It is clearly a never ending and pointed, in a experienced sense but also in a definitional sense, towards the absolute other.

The question of whether the Other has Desire towards an absolutely other subjective to itself presents two obvious options. Yes the Other has Desire. No the Other doesn’t have Desire or its not possible to know if the Other has Desire. The second interpretation that the Other does not have Desire is not obviously wrong: to define the Other as having anything could violate exactly what makes the Other other and thus Levinas’s power core of metaphysical pluralism. “For Levinas...desire is the respect and knowledge of the other as other, the ethico-metaphysical moment whose transgression consciousness must forbid itself. According to Hegel, on the contrary, this gesture of transgression and assimilation is necessary and essential. (Derrida, p115) For this paper the claim will be that the Other can be thematized as desiring an absolutely other.  As it has been described this thematization is done under the softest hand possible with a acceptance of continual totality. Now what does the Other desire? This will be called ‘the New’. 

It makes sense that Levinas would not use the term ‘the New’ when discussing the absolutely other because it invokes too closely the concept of the news in newspapers and news radio. It is intuitive that everybody wants/desires to know the news of the world they care about in general but mass-media mediums are totalizing spigots without discourse. They are a pathetic substitute for conversation (although many people use it as such). However this term is useful here because it can be used to describe what it means for the Other to have Desire from the Other’s subjective viewpoint. Instead of having to write ‘The absolute other of the Absolute Other is absolutely other to the me more so in that the I doesn’t know it desires it.’ it is possible to write...

The Other is always motivated by Desire to the New and the I’s only access to  the New is through the absolutely other. This is the second serious thematization of the Other presented here. Since ethics/love comes before wisdom then the truth of the New can only be known, even the very kind of question it is, through the Desire that the Other has. 

All this may be a round-about way of saying that the passions and interests of the Other can be interesting in a way the I can’t know beforehand. Given that Desire is always insatiable yet capable of some gratification in its grasping of the absolutely other it would be legitimate to say that the greatest gratification is to be found in the New of the Other. The best way to satisfy the insatiable desire for the absolutely other is to drink from the absolutely other of the absolutely Other! This has intuitive weight because often I find I am most satisfied when socializing with Others and they are telling me their interests (and I care, if I’ve decided by my arbitrary freedom that I don’t care then there is little gratification but that’s another more complicated story) 

The Other’s Desire for the New is never ending and thus produces a suffering for the Other. This is not a situational claim. No matter the food just eaten by an Other or their luxurious surroundings it is known, metaphysically/totally, that they are suffering for the New. This concept of suffering is different from the standard everyday concept of suffering wherein the person lacks a dwelling or is hungry or is bereaved, etc. The standard concept of suffering will come later and more maturely given the stronger understanding of suffering of the Other as absolute previous to any comparison within a metaphysics.

#### Previous to comparing Others’s suffering there is hostageness

It has been established that all Others are always suffering for the New. It is important then to establish that there is no method to compare sufferings for the New between Others. All Others are always suffering and are incomparable thus unique in their suffering. Because of this to make sufferings comparable would require a totalizing process. Previous to any totalizing process the I is hostage to the incomparable sufferings of the Other and Others. 

Since Desire is always having a noetic direction the I goes to the Suffering of the Other to see what the Other is looking at. What their interest is. The implications of such relation are impressive. 

Consider this one: So the ontological structure of ethics is an incomparable and absolute relation from which one satisfies the appetite for the insatiable by the interests of the other. From that it is then obvious that, considering that those interests might contradict each other “Goodness in the subject is anarchy itself” (Otherwise, p135).  So having Others able to pursue, in freedom, their interests enables greater access to the New. Thus the I is responsible to the Other’s freedom for ontic access. This is just as Levinas describes “As a responsibility for the freedom of the other, it is prior to any freedom for me, but it also precedes any violence in me, which would be contrary of freedom.” (Otherwise, p135) But not just responsibility: joy from the great gratification of drinking in the double absolute other of the New for the Other.

Consider that for Husserl the epoche, the grounding for great knowledge, required isolation and reflection whereas now great knowledge is a social event, experienced through openness and interest in the Other’s passions. A criticism of this could be that this is a complete following of the mob. That one would be completely lost without the crowd. Well when the crowd when bad is a mob and when good is a community. This is Levinas’s metaphysical claim. That we live in these crowds. And we should be worried about being duped by the mob. (And because of this work Levinas is the greatest Metaphysician of the 20th century)

Consider the joy of living as an Other for Others

The joy! I spend my days working the elemental where I take a bite out of the earth in enjoyment and find gratification for my insatiable desires through the passions of the Others around me. Not by conquering because violence silences the absolute other but by asking with passivity what the Other’s interest are and then engaging in discourse. Now there is also that they as Others (if we allow a third thematization) desire to know my interests and passions, not out of some sort of contractual agreement that I be interested in their stuff and in return they be interested in mine, but out of the very metaphysics of our existence. The Other has desire for my alterity to them.

Consider the the Beggar again. Before the beggar scenario was a failure of Levinas because we need to sleep or we need to take care of ourselves or we want to be allowed to be selfish for its own sake sometimes. And the beggar is always asking for more from us which we cannot or don’t want to give. But now failure is not assured! If failure exists in the beggar scenario it is the lack of discourse due to over-thematization. A beggar is not an absolute Other if the beggar is thematized as not being free. Someone who is hungry, someone who is in fear, is muzzled and their alterity buried by their exile (by knowledge known) during the walking over, before the face-to-face, that is carried over. The true failure of the beggar scenario is to KNOW the person is a beggar. There is an Other, absolutely Other, that should be approached openly and with Desire neoticially pointed to the New of the Other. And although this is a goal at which one will often fail, because the forces of objectification are strong and party with naive naturalism which is the dominate metaphysics, it is not necessary that failure will always occur. The Beggar can never be helped for even if a beggar is cleaned up, put in a suit and trained in the ways of business the person will always be a Beggar-turned-person instead of the Other that in freedom made significations of their wellness.

There is so much to experience especially considering that Levinas’s metaphysics show there is unbreakable fecundity with all others. The desires and interests of others demanded of the I. The interests of the I can by signification become interests of Others (talking about your hobbies is not a cost upon your friends). There is an obligation to the Other and vice-versa so the Other probably won’t reject the I. Experience is richer because now the lived world is a proximitial world (not a static platonic one) where the I is involved with the Other in the infinity and thus without boredom. Rejecting over-thematized ethical systems means that a difficult problem is not necessarily solvable, there is no absolute justice in the world, but that something can always be done, especially listening. 

It is important to note that a Levinasian ethical metaphysics that is impossible for Derrida because of how language requires a totality. “A pure thought of pure difference. Empiricism is its philosophical name, its metaphysical pretention or modesty. We say the dream because it must vanish at daybreak, as soon as language awakens.” (Derrida, p189) Still though Derrida was Levinas’s most famous supporter. So Derrida saw the value in this approach. And the author needs to read Derrida more.

Of the implications the final one considered will be a contemporarly important one: There is also an implicit rejection of history as ethical progress because to know history is progress requires a measure of all the sufferings of the Others in a totalizing metaphysics that drowns out the voice of the Other. Where is the progress when each person’s suffering is unique and incomparable? History as progress must then be redefined as progress in technology, sciences and not as progress in satisfying Desire. This is especially important because rejecting history-as-process is one of the great tasks of philosophy today.

#### Possible Future Work

This paper has shown that the hostageness of the other over the I is a wonderful insight, a wonderful joy because of the continual discovery it allows. Given greater space there are plentiful future avenues of work building on including how the metaphysics of Levinas provide an excellent platform for understanding the costs of war by describing war as unnatural (not the basic state of affairs) and counting the possible dimensions of absolute alterity lost to death. Of course each death kills uncountable discourses but Aristotelians need their numbers. Currently under aristotelian metaphysics the victor of the war decides the ethical framework. And war is the only way to peace. But with Levinas it is not so. 

Also this metaphysical foundation allows for a discussion about how best to begin the metaphysical act of judging levels of suffering as comparable? There are beggars in the world and institutions that feed them require significations of the support they deserve.

It is important to consider Levinas within a metaphysical pluralism. That means that Levinas doesn’t want any project to dominate discussion and thus even his own. This raises an irony that I’ve tried to respond to a criticism of Levinas that implies pluralism outside ethical metaphysics is needed (which is good) by arguing that the criticism is unwarranted (which would obviate the need for pluralism in this case) Also from this pluralism platform it is possible to approach Levinas’s work as a metaphysical logos, even if it claims logically contradictory axioms. For sure claiming something is not axiomatic doesn’t mean its not axiomatic. But given the self-reflective nature of Levinas’s metaphysical claims things get murky pretty fast when trying to build a foundation. And yet given all the dirt Levinas’s work is a most excellent song. 

## Conclusion

The concept of being held hostage to the Other’s suffering should be understood as a metaphysical fact that precedes absolutely any thematization of the Other-as-a-Sufferer so that the primordial source of the hostageness is the alterity of the Other. This was shown by first grounding the discussion of Levinas, as required, outside the Aristotelian metaphysical framework of wisdom before love. Instead we have love before truth and thereafter reason birthed from discourse. This is a radical move and it allows for a radical reorientation away from the failures of Western Philosophy, most specifically the totalization of Being, and to a metaphysical pluralism birthed in proximality and subjective experience. 

From this framework the concept of being held hostage to the Other can sometimes be misunderstood as being held hostage to the suffering of the Other when the Other is actually really suffering i.e. they are hungry, starving, ignored, hated, etc. The needs of the Other in this position, being endless, will always exhaust the finite ability of the I to respond to suffering. Both the supporting and criticizing arguments assume that two thematizations. Firstly that the ethical relation is absolute and always taking from the I or giving to the Other. Secondly that the Other is suffering because I am strong in my dwelling. Both these thematizations perform an unnecessary violence against the Other and should be rejected. The actualization of that failure is demonstrated in the scenario of the beggar on the street where it is shown the beggar should not be approached as a beggar.

There was a better position considered wherein there is a refusal to thematize the Other as a sometimes-Sufferer-sometimes-not before the ethical relation is established. This move was supported by a review of the some of Levinas’s moves against thematization, a constant theme in Totality and Otherwise.

The Other is show to be necessary to the best joy of the world by slow and deliberate thematizations of the Other. The first Thematization of the Other is that it is absolutely Other (the weakest possible said). This is (at least should be) uncontroversial and obvious to Levinas readers. The second thematization of the Other as an Other that has Desire for the New. This is implicitly part of Levinas’s metaphysics. This leads to a reconsidering of the joy of living as an Other for Others where every encounter with an Other has by metaphysical support both an absolute interest in the projects, works and passions of the Other and the Other has the same interest in the I. There is so much to experience under the unbreakable fecundity with all others. The I’s desires and interests are the Other’s (talking about your hobbies is not a cost upon your friends) and the Other’s passions the I has desire to know. It reaffirms the rejection of history as (ethical) progress, the most important work of philosophy today and provides an avenue for future work.

Levinas’s work is such beautiful thought. And yet there is a painful irony: to spend hours away from friends and others writing how great friends and others are! I suffer!
