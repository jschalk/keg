Great Philosopher/World Historical Philosopher: Levinas

UTEP Spring 2014

Dr. Jules Simon

Essay #1

Your paper should be typed, double-spaced and thoroughly proofread and edited. It will be graded for both quality of content and quality of writing, that is, for the originality and depth of your analyses and interpretations and the effectiveness and clarity of your writing. Since this is a senior-, graduate-level course, my expectation of each of you is accordingly very high and I am looking forward to working with you as you further develop your writing and analytical skills. Your paper should be an original work of direct interpretation of the text, approximately 10 pages long. For senior-level students (and other undergraduates), you must include at least two other significant sources as part of your research. This could entail either using major commentators as secondary sources or comparing the work of other philosophers to that of Levinas’s. You must integrate several direct quotes from the primary text as well as adequate secondary sources to indicate your familiarity with the issues raised in the texts. Each graduate student must at least use three other significant sources either for support of your thesis or for comparative purposes with a paper length of approximately 15 pages. If possible, your thesis or for comparative purposes with a paper length of approximately 15 pages. If possible, your work should be aligned with me after you finish your first draft in order to discuss your writing and the extent of your involvement in the course.

Choose one of the following topics to write on: 

1. For Levinas, “Ethics is First Philosophy.” For Simon, Levina’s philosophy exemplifies ‘phenomenological ethics.’ Use a concrete, contemporary event in your personal life or social relations in general (politics, community developments, school situation, etc.) and apply several of Levinas’s key assertions to evaluate the ethical character of your chosen event as a Levinasian. For example, you should include the concepts totalization and infinition as well as ‘the other’ and ‘the same.’ In addition, you could include conceptualizations such as subjectivity, objectivity, sensibility, responsibility, the interior, exteriority, the face discourse, the face-to-face, the body, need, desire, welcome, dwelling, femininity, paternity, eros, love, fecundity, possession, war or peace. Your approach should exemplify that of phenomenological ethics. (Simon, posted on blackboard)
