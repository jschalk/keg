Other’s command over me. 
Dr. Simon discussed how, for Levinas, I allow the Other to command me because I’m already in the position to be able to command. Hence I can listen to the Other in safety and security to understand the needs of the Other. Levinas is taken to task by lots of people/philosophers for his insistence ofn theanthe asymmetricalasymmetrical ness of the ethical relationship, as if I live for the Other. But However, this attack is unfair because, for Levinas, first you must be in command before you can command the Other to command you. I experience the autonomy of freedom.

Epistemological Foundations: is the natural first state of man war? 
Levinas starts from an anti-Marxist position, a rejection of History=class struggle, the classic confrontational battle. Levinas suggests that we need to develop our own autonomy and not necessarily enter the class struggle. Such movement develops a liberal metaphysics of autonomy.  Levinas undermines the dialectics of war. On the contrary, “for Marx there are no faces”. Dr Simon told us that a colleague said to have studied Heidegger to give a face to his Marxist position.Levinas presents an alternative to the dialectics of War. There is no state of constant Wars. We might be duped to morality. Marx says that the only way to think about history is as class struggle. Marx undermines this Hegelian version because it is ideological: there are no Faces. We have to develop a healthy autonomy to affirm liberal orientation AND we have to be aware how how its good to be alive. LevinasHeMarx talks about this not for the sake of ‘my right’ to do this or that (possibly a liberal agenda). For Levinas this is how experience is!. We live by nourishing ourselves!. Not for the sake of collecting possessions and then fighting over them. This is how we learn to want others to enjoy life, to sacrifice for Others. In Totality and Infinity, Levinas is setting us up for the sociality of experience: damn it’s good to be alive, and damn it’s wrong that you are suffering.

Responsibility is the purpose of Otherwise and Being. 
For Levinas, sensibility is a prior condition to rationl systems. First I hear the suffering of the Other, and then I develop a rationlity through conversation. Perception (Levinas loved Maurice Merleau-Ponty) is important because we live as embodied beings in the world. There is a difference in that Merleau-Ponty focuses on disfunction, but Levinas focuses on enjoyment.

Western Philosophy is more concerned with Being or Becoming. In this manner Levinas is giving us a way in writing to get started. 

Survival Metaphysics 
Dr Simon described how in a survival metaphysics, I take care of myself first. I take care of the Other only because they will take care of me. This is the logic of the United Nations: to protect the sovereignty of the member states. Yet there a degrees of severity to which this notion is enforced. For every member involved in a totalizing state of war, Ccommerce is better than War. We’re able to get to the point of commerce “and yet we must ask if…we must not presuppose this… Presupposses the breathlessness of the spirit…” For Levinas Trade is an example of successful rationlity. However,But he wants to entertain that we don’t have to be conditioned to think that all of our life is Commerce and War. We do not always have to secure the most oxygen for ourselves. This is so Bbecause we can hold our breath. (There is a long tradition in Jewish tradition for talking about breath.)

Guillermo’s Personal note:
The introduction of the term expiation in the last reading section really struck me. I have known that in Christian theology expiation refers to the death of Christ in the cross for the sins of the world, avoiding in this manner divine retribution. In Levinas, expiation seems to take a deeper although similar meaning. It does not start as an initiative or as an effect of my own virtue. It starts when I am thrown back upon myself and I am put in the place of another, I can conceive his subjectivity but not in a form of transubstantiation but as a substitution for another. This means that I can suffer the other’s suffering and in that same instance question my powers. In the same manner, I can enjoy when the other is enjoying, but not at an emotional, but at an inter-subjective level. Levinas’ philosophy has opened my eyes to a new reality in a much unexpected way, and has confronted me by asking: When have you sacrificed yourself for others? I wonder how difficult is to exercise this kind of piety, and definitively I want to put some of it into practice.  I am convinced this goes far way beyond simple empathy and I am really excited to discover what’s coming next!
