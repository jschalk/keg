Amongst contemporary French philosophers, Emmanuel Levinas is one of the most influential, having directed attention in an original and creative way to ethical theory and practice by raising the question of how I measure my responsibility to the Other. For Levinas, my ethical responsibility to others is measure by recourse to the concept of the infinite and to challenging the structures of totalization, especially in the context of modern culture and modern philosophy. He works out his theory in two seminal texts upon which we will focus: Totality and Infinity and Otherwise Than Being. One of our tasks will be to determine in what way Levinas comes up with the claim that “ethics is first philosophy” and how he develops that claim across these two texts. The other task will be to explore the extent to which these texts both emerged from Levinas’s experience of exile and the ways in which the language of ‘exile’ forms an essential core of his writing projects.

In studying Totality and Infinity we will explore Levinas’s phenomenological investigations of: metaphysics, transcendence, truth, justice, speech as discourse, enjoyment, labor, representation, the mythical, the dwelling (home), the body, freedom, the gift, the face, the other, the Other, love, eros, fecundity, time, and desire. Levinas dedicated Otherwise than Being to the Jews who died in the Holocaust, including this inscription on the title page:

> To the memory of those who were closest among the six million assassinated by the National Socialists, and of the millions on millions of all confessions and all nations, victims of the same hatred of the other man, the same anti-semitism.

Levinas contends that to avoid ethical insensibility we must become more morally sensible, that is, we must become ethical subjects. In what has become that mark of his ethical philosophy, Levinas claims that we must be brought to the point of “substitution for the other” in such a radical way that I become hostage to and for the other, for the other’s needs, for their life, for their responsibility-indeed, to the degree that I become ‘my brother’s keeper’ absolutely. The way that such a condition is cultivated is through learning about the proximity of the other as my neighbor to the extent that I ‘learn’ to suffer for the suffering of the other. 

Required texts: Emmanuel Levinas, Totality and Infinity and Otherwise Than Being or Beyond Essence

Course Policies and Grading

Presentations and Participation: 20%<br>
I will only be taking attendance the first few meetings in order to better familiarize myself with your names and faces. Attendance is not mandatory but I strongly recommend your frequent and involved participation. You will be graded, however, on some concrete indicators of participation: each of you will be responsible for leading the discussion for two of the reading assignments, each of which for undergraduates will count for 10% of your grade. That is because each graduate student will be assigned to mentor an undergraduate student and must turn in a one-page description of their mentoring relationship at the end of the semester which will count for 10% of their grade. 

Protocol: 10%<br>
You will also be expected to write out the class notes (one to two single-spaced pages) for one of the class sessions. This aspect of your work is meant to facilitate a sense of continuity and coherence to our meetings. You might be assigned a partner with whom you will type up the respective notes and hand out copies to the rest of the class. Since each member of the class will be responsible for this assignment for at least one time, you will incur the copying costs for your particular turn. 

Papers: 70% (35 points each) <br>
Since this is a writing-intensive course, two papers will be assigned which will constitute the greatest part of your grade. Each paper should be typed, double-spaced and thoroughly proofread and edited. They will be graded for both quality of content and quality of writing, that is, for the originality and depth of your analyses and interpretations and effectiveness and clarity of your writing. Since this is a senior-graduate-level course, my expectation of each of you is accordingly very high and I am looking forward to working with you as you further develop your writing and analytical skills. Each paper should be an original work of direct interpretation of the text, approximately 10 pages long. For senior-level students (and other undergraduates), you must include at least two significant sources as part of your research. This could entail either using major commentators as secondary sources of comparing the work of other philosophers to that of Levinas’s. Each graduate student must use at least three other significant sources either for support of your thesis or for comparative purposes with a purposes with a paper length of approximately 15 pages. If possible, your work should be aligned with your MA thesis work or be good enough quality to present at a professional workshop of conference. Each student will be scheduled for a mandatory meeting with me after you finish your first paper in order to discuss your writing and the extent of your involvement in the course.
