Summary: Mr. King Dunlap, American football player, told a story about two football mates of his getting into an argument and his inability to know what to do. 

American football is a violent game of passions and so instead of talking things out when there is a conflict it is often more acceptable to fight. Not knowing who to fight or at least posture to fight is unacceptably shameful. Normally who to fight is decided by a strict heirarchy of informal rules. Most scenarios involve fighting the other squad (different color uniform) or "the other side of the ball" ala the Defense vs Offense. However in this scenario the two people fighting were Philip Rivers and Nick Hardwick. One is the most important leader of the squad, who Dunlap must protect often at the cost of his own body, and the other is the leader of his group who he must listen to in all situations. They have gotten into an argument and Mr. Rivers headbutts Mr. Hardwick. King must decide in an instance whether to attack Philip for assaulting Nick, attack Nick cause he's the enemy, or put himself inbetween them to force them to stop fighting. Which would be antitheatical to his commitment to follow their lead. Another person on the podcast describes the dilemma this way:

> Who do you ride with?
