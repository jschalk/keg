# Sean called

**Date:** 2026-09-25  
**Author:** Emmanuel Schalk       

> *They are the best managed companies in America*

---

I have a retirement account with 10 thousand dollars in it. A few months ago, probably in March, a stock broker named Sean called me and asked me how I wanted to invest it. We talked for 30 minutes. He took his time and listened patiently.

<div align="center">

### This was completely wild to me
</div>

<div align="center"><img src="images/suprise_face_from_charles_bell_anatomy.webp" alt="look of surprise and fear" width="500"></div>

How on earth was it worth Sean's time to talk to me? To pay his salary and overhead he needs to be going after retirement accounts with a ton more money. Never before had a finance person talked to me for so long.[^1] 

<div align="center">

### I've never been this important before.<br> So what was suddenly different?
</div>


Sean was assigned my account by a larger company. Why was assigned my account? Probably because he's not a great stock broker. If he was great he would be investing himself with his own hedge fund. And it's not like he could beat the fancy trading algorithms.[^2]  

Sean's not a Numerical Science guy, he's a talker. He's a good talker. He good at engaging with me. He askes about my interests and plans. He's attentive in his listening. He's fun. Sean's probably a former frat boy. Fraternities are underrated in how useful they are. Going to parties, learning how to have a good time with people. How to talk to them and help them enjoy the moment. Very useful training for being a stock broker. Sean's profession involves talking to people and making them feel good. 

<div align="center">

### Sean wants to make me feel good so he can sell.
</div>


But what is he selling? He's talking about stock profolio plans and risk profiles. He offers a conservative investing strategy, an aggressive strategy, and a middle one. But no matter what I chose he will be making no money off me. 

<div align="center">

### I know he's not selling me investment strategies. <br>I'm not the farmer I'm the animal. He's milking me. 
</div>

My fund is too small. I'm pretty sure hes flooding my head with confusing options so that my guard will be down. If I'm trying to decide what risk profile I am and what industry I want to invest in I won't notice when he hits me with the hammer. What he really wants. I don't believe in Sean one bit but talking on the phone isn't something I do often. I don't know how to play these games well.   

Here's my conclusion about what he really wanted: 

<div align="center">

### Hes doing general marketing for AI companies.  
</div>

After 15 minutes of building trust he started dropping this line: 

>*These AI companies are the best managed companies in America.*

This is a total lie. There is no way Sean could know if they are well managed or how well all other companies in America are managed. All the articles I read about AI indicates that they are run by falible humans who because of tremendous market pressure and greed completely turn away from all honesty and goodwill. They are not the best managed companies in America. They are building a product everyone hates and no one will pay for. 

Their business model is going to fail. They will never raise enough revenue to justify the capital they raised, it's likely they will go bankrupt and their stocks will crash. They are running the pump and dump as long as they can. 

It's possible that Sean is an idiot. But I'm not getting that. Sean was a smart talker and capable of responding to my questions attentively. He might be a useful idiot but cynicism in American business is almost absolute. It's unlikely that he doesn't know what he's doing.  

Because he could be paid to help me pick my investment strategy (no money) or he could be paid to advertise AI stocks (actual money).

<div align="center">

### Somehow the AI companies are paying Sean to pump up their stock.
</div>

There are legal ways to pay brokers to violate their fundicary obligations to their clients. We've all seen Wolf of Wall Street[^3]. The AI companies are using all the tools they have to keep making money. Sean is one of those tools. The more Sean markets to people like me, even if I don't have any money others I talk with might. The constant lies are very simple: the more they lie the more money they make. 

One day the crash will come. If I invest in AI stocks maybe I'll make $1000 this year he'll get $100. However when the crash comes I'll lose 9k and some of that 9k will go to Sean's bosses. That's how Sean's milking me. 

Thats how the Seans of the world are milking their neighbors for the AI companies.

<div align="center">

### The Seans of the world are pumping AI stocks.<br>Somebody is buying the lies.
### Probably old rich people.
</div>

And this gives at least one reason why the crash hasn't come yet.

Assume 10 hours working per day: 1k / 10 = $100 every hour. 

Sean needs to bring an average of $100 per hour if he wants to be a low paid stock broker.
