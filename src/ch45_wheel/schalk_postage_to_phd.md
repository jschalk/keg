## A Philosopher Speaks and Writes

*What it means to be a Philosopher* is a big question so let's concentrate on one thing: how a Philosopher should speak/write to his audience. 

Given a subject a philosopher should be able to speak/write to an audience in any given time frame and as much as possible in the language of the audience.

### Common Examples of Time Frame/Subject/Audience

15 minutes to talk about ethics to engineers at construction company<br>
10 minutes to introduce research project to colleagues<br>
30 seconds in pitch research proposal to a supervisor <br>
50 minutes to introduce a philosopher's thought to a high school class<br>
26 lectures of 50 minutes on subject to undergrads (13 week course) <br> 

### Common Examples of Word Count/Subject/Audience

500 words to describe book to friends online<br>
3 pages max to introduce research project to committee<br>
10 word blurb to describe class to prospective students<br>
1 chapter about a philosopher's ethical framework for book for Environmental Activists<br>
300 page book on Art and Responsiblity for those interested in justice <br>     

### The expected range

Generally, as a philosopher you must be able to connect your audience with your expertise given the circumstances. It is your responsiblity that the material be as accesible as possible.[^1]

| Type              | Structure              |
| ----------------- | ---------------------- |
| Slogan            | <5 words               |
| Sentence          | <10 words              |
| Elevator pitch    | <40 words              |
| 5-minute talk     | <100 words             |
| 30-minute talk    | <1,000 words           |
| 1.5-hour lecture  | <3,000 words           |
| 3-hour lecture    | <6,000 words           |
| 2-day seminar     | <20,000 words          |
| University course | 13 lectures, ~26 hours |
| Master’s degree   | ~2 years               |
| Public book       | ~50,000–100,000 words  |
| PhD               | ~5 years               |
[^2]

This is what I learned from Simon.[^3] Hopefully I got it right. When Simon explained this to us (the students) he was claim and charming. Thats what his audience needed. He lived the teaching.
